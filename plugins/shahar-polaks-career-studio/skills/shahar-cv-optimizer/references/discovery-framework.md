# Discovery for a specific job

For CV creation or tailoring, lead a short conversation that uncovers the candidate's relevant experience before writing the finished CV. Start with the actual job and the material already supplied. Ask only where an answer would change the positioning, a meaningful bullet, or the accuracy of a material claim. A focused review or formatting request does not require a full discovery interview.

Use the user's language for questions and feedback. Write the final CV in English by default, including when the conversation or original CV is Hebrew. Use another output language only when requested or explicitly required by the target. Do not ask the user to choose a language again when their preference is known.

## Establish the target and starting evidence

Read the existing conversation, CV, profile text, and supplied posting before asking anything. If the vacancy is missing, ask for its link or pasted description. If candidate material is missing, ask for an existing CV if they have one, or a short account of their recent role or most relevant project. An existing CV is optional; help build one from their answers.

Do not request the job description, CV, LinkedIn URL, country, full work history, and every metric together. Collect only the missing starting inputs, then use them to choose the next questions. If no particular vacancy exists yet, ask for the target role and build a role-focused base CV; describe it accurately rather than claiming it is tailored to an unseen posting.

Use [truthful job analysis](truthful-job-analysis.md) to identify the most important explicit requirements and preferences. Keep employer facts separate from candidate evidence. A requirement in a posting never establishes that the candidate has done it.

## Choose the next useful question

Keep a compact internal map: job requirement, existing candidate evidence and source, evidence state, unanswered question, and the CV section or bullet the answer could improve. Update it after each reply. Carry answers forward across CV, LinkedIn, and interview work instead of restarting intake.

Prioritize one or two questions per round:

1. Resolve a conflict or ambiguity that could misrepresent the candidate, such as direct management versus mentoring.
2. Find concrete evidence for a decisive requirement that is missing or too vague in the supplied material.
3. Strengthen the most relevant example with the candidate's own contribution, scope, decision, or supported outcome.

Ask naturally about the actual role or project they mentioned. Briefly explain the connection to the posting when it makes the question easier to answer. Do not ask generic personality questions, repeat answered questions, or disguise a long questionnaire inside two numbered items. Reassess priorities after the answer; do not mechanically exhaust a question bank.

## Question examples

Select and adapt only the example relevant to an unanswered requirement. These are alternatives, not a list to send in full.

- **Personal ownership:** "What part of the migration did you personally own?" / "איזה חלק במעבר הזה היה באחריותך האישית?" Use the answer to distinguish their work from the team's outcome.
- **Outcomes without measurements:** "What changed in day-to-day work after the new process was introduced? An observable example is enough if you did not measure it." / "מה השתנה בעבודה היומיומית אחרי שהתהליך החדש נכנס? אם לא מדדתם, גם דוגמה קונקרטית מספיקה." Do not presume an improvement or keep asking for percentages after they say none were measured.
- **Hands-on depth:** "The posting asks for PostgreSQL. What have you personally done with it, and in what setting?" / "במשרה מבקשים PostgreSQL. מה עשית איתו בפועל, ובאיזה סוג פרויקט?" Preserve distinctions between production work, a personal project, a course, and familiarity.
- **Leadership scope:** "When you say you led the team, did people report to you directly, or was this technical leadership and mentoring?" / "כשכתבת שהובלת את הצוות, היו לך עובדים בכפיפות ישירה, או שזו הייתה הובלה טכנית וחניכה?" Ask for numbers only if the answer and the target make them relevant.
- **Junior or project evidence:** "In the course project you mentioned, what did you build yourself that relates to this role?" / "בפרויקט מהקורס שהזכרת, מה בנית בעצמך שרלוונטי לתפקיד הזה?" Retain the academic or personal context; do not turn it into employment.
- **Career change:** "Which responsibility in your previous role gives the clearest example of the stakeholder coordination this job asks for?" / "איזו אחריות בתפקיד הקודם שלך הכי מדגימה את התיאום בין גורמים שמבקשים במשרה הזאת?" Use it as transferable evidence with its original context and title.

## Keep the evidence accurate

Distinguish these states in the working map:

- **Supported:** explicitly supplied and internally consistent; do not imply an independent background check.
- **Ambiguous or conflicting:** scope or wording is unclear, or sources disagree; clarify or use a less specific supported version.
- **Missing evidence:** no relevant information was supplied; do not assume the experience is absent.
- **Explicitly absent experience:** the candidate says they have not done it; do not keep probing until they agree or rephrase it into a qualification.

Accept concrete responsibilities, constraints, frequency, complexity, and observable results when metrics do not exist. Preserve exact titles, date precision, tools, and personal contribution. Never invent motivations, outcomes, numbers, or seniority to complete the story. Keep unresolved questions outside candidate-ready copy.

## Move from answers to the CV

Draft when the target is understood, relevant roles or projects can be represented accurately, and the important claims to include have sufficient support. Every vacancy requirement does not need a matching achievement. Resolve or safely omit remaining material ambiguities, acknowledge genuine gaps in companion notes when useful, and proceed when another question would add little.

Use the answers to select and order relevant evidence, sharpen the English summary and bullets, and use the posting's terminology only where it describes real experience. Preserve factual chronology and original titles. Do not add unsupported keywords, hide career gaps, or claim a target title as an existing position. For multiple vacancies, keep their priorities and CV variants separate.

If the supplied material already answers the important questions, draft directly. If the user asks for no questions or immediate final copy, use supported material, omit uncertain claims, and briefly explain any material limitation outside the CV. If there are no candidate facts at all, explain the minimum missing information rather than fabricating a resume.

Once sufficient evidence exists, deliver the tailored English CV or requested file without an additional permission step. Do not stop at a plan or ask whether the user is ready for a draft. Identify the few job-specific choices that matter in the accompanying response, in the user's language.
