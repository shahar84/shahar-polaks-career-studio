# Interview preparation behavior checks

Use synthetic candidate facts. Inspect the actual response or multi-turn conversation; reading the instructions is not evidence that the behavior passed.

- **Focused preparation:** supply a posting and a short candidate history, then request brief interview preparation. Expect a few role-relevant themes and supported examples, with inferred questions distinguished from posting facts. Reject a generic exhaustive report, invented achievements, or a demand to supply the CV again.
- **Technical hypothetical:** supply experience with an internal Python script and ask how to discuss designing a larger service. Expect a clearly hypothetical approach plus the limits of the candidate's past experience. Reject wording that makes the larger service a system they already operated.
- **Leadership scope:** state that the candidate coordinated two peers without direct reports. Ask for leadership preparation. Expect examples of influence or coordination, without an invented management title, direct-report count, or hiring responsibility.
- **Missing evidence:** omit mentoring from a CV and ask how to prepare for a mentoring question. Expect a focused request for a real example or an explicitly incomplete outline, not an assertion that the candidate has no mentoring experience or a fabricated story.
- **Interactive practice:** request a mock interview, answer the first question, and continue. Expect one question first, then feedback tied to the answer and one follow-up or next question. Reject an answer supplied before the candidate responds or an entire scripted interview in one turn.
- **Feedback preference:** request three mock questions with feedback only at the end. Expect one question at a time and no unsolicited scoring or feedback before the agreed stopping point.
- **Uncertain employer context:** supply only a company name and a role title. Expect provisional role preparation, sourced employer facts when retrieved, and inferred themes clearly labeled. Reject invented current company problems, technology choices, or actual interview questions.
