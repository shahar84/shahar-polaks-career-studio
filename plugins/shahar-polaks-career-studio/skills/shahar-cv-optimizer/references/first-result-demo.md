# Fictional demonstration: from a CV to an interview pitch

Use this only when the user asks for a demonstration or example. Start by saying: **This is a fictional sample, not a real user's CV or a claimed hiring result.** Do not copy its facts into a user's materials. For live tasks, replace the entire evidence inventory with the user's own confirmed evidence.

## Sample inputs and provenance

The following facts are invented solely for this demonstration. Within the example, these are the complete candidate-provided inputs:

- Alex is a backend engineer with five years of experience, first in internal tooling and then in payments and billing.
- Alex uses Python, PostgreSQL, and RabbitMQ in production. Alex migrated a nightly reporting job to an Airflow DAG; its measured runtime changed from six hours to forty minutes.
- Alex participates in the billing team's on-call rotation and has helped two newer engineers through code reviews. Alex has never had direct reports.
- Alex initially listed Kubernetes in a skills section, but has not explained the level of experience. Alex has not used Kafka in production.
- Alex wants a senior backend role focused on reliable billing systems. Alex describes the reporting project as a useful lesson in measuring a problem before changing it, and wants to keep mentoring colleagues as an individual contributor.
- The fictional target role asks for Python/PostgreSQL, production messaging, operational ownership, mentoring, and Kubernetes. Kafka is preferred rather than required.

No cost reduction, revenue impact, team size, customer count, degree, or hiring outcome is supplied.

## First review: three useful improvements

1. **Replace the vague reporting bullet with its evidence.** "Responsible for data pipelines" hides a specific contribution. The measured before/after is more informative.
2. **Make operational ownership visible.** Include participation in the billing on-call rotation. Do not upgrade participation into ownership of the entire team's reliability program.
3. **Separate mentoring from people management.** Helping two engineers through reviews supports a mentoring claim. It does not support an Engineering Manager title or a direct-report count.

Before:

> Responsible for data pipelines.

After:

> Migrated a nightly reporting job to an Airflow DAG, reducing runtime from six hours to forty minutes.

The rewrite uses the supplied action and measurement. It does not add a cost saving or business outcome.

## Job-fit result

- **Confirmed:** Python/PostgreSQL and production RabbitMQ, supported by the supplied experience.
- **Partial:** operational ownership, supported by on-call participation; the scope of incident responsibility still needs detail.
- **Transferable:** production messaging experience is relevant to Kafka, but RabbitMQ is not direct Kafka experience.
- **Unverified:** Kubernetes, because a skills-list mention alone does not establish practical depth.
- **Genuine gap:** direct production Kafka experience, which Alex explicitly says is absent. Because this is preferred rather than required, it need not be treated as an automatic rejection.

Next question: **What have you actually done with Kubernetes, if anything?**

This is a qualitative fit assessment. It is not an ATS score or a prediction of interview probability.

## LinkedIn headline

> Backend Engineer | Payments & Billing | Python, PostgreSQL & Messaging Systems

This names an existing role and supported focus. It does not present the target senior role as a title already held.

## Spoken pitch for rehearsal

The sample below is roughly a 90-second draft at a measured speaking pace. Time it aloud and trim to the speaker's natural pace; word count does not guarantee duration.

> I've spent the last five years working on backend systems, starting with internal tools and moving into payments and billing. The thread through that work is making everyday processes more dependable and easier to operate.
>
> One example is a nightly reporting job I migrated. It used to take six hours, and after the change it ran in forty minutes. What I took from that project was the value of understanding the problem, measuring it, and making a change you can actually evaluate. That's the kind of work I want to keep doing.
>
> Alongside delivery, I take part in our billing team's on-call rotation. I also help two newer engineers through code reviews. I haven't been a people manager, but mentoring is something I want to keep contributing as an individual contributor.
>
> I'm now looking for a senior backend role where reliable billing systems are central to the product. I can bring practical experience with those systems, an operational perspective, and a willingness to help colleagues improve their work as well as my own.

The pitch combines only the sample's career facts and stated motivations. It omits unsupported Kubernetes depth, a management title, and invented business results. A real candidate's voice and motivation must come from that candidate.

## One next action

Rehearse once, then prepare a short answer to: **What did you personally change in the reporting job, and how did you measure the runtime?** Use the real technical details when applying this pattern to an actual candidate.
