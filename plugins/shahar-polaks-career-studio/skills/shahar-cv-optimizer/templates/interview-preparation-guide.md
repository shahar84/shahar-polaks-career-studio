# Interview preparation guide

Use only the preparation the user requested. Follow the [interview preparation workflow](../references/interview-preparation.md), reuse existing candidate evidence, and remove empty sections. The template supplies structure, never candidate facts or a claim about the employer's actual interview process.

For a general request, keep the first pass compact: the most important role themes, relevant stories, any decisive gap, and two or three useful employer questions. For a single answer or mock interview, use that requested format directly.

## Role themes

Select up to three or four themes most material to the role. Identify which come from supplied requirements and which are preparation inferences. If only a role title is available, say the selection is provisional. Do not present inferred practice questions as confirmed employer questions.

For each theme, provide a likely practice question, the strongest supported example to prepare, and the one decision or contribution the candidate should make clear. If evidence is absent, ask a focused evidence question or identify the confirmed gap; do not fill the slot with an invented story.

## Story preparation

Route personal pitches and behavioral or STAR answers to the bundled [interview-pitch skill](../../interview-pitch/SKILL.md). Give it the existing evidence, target, and open questions. Do not ask the user to repeat their CV. Separate what the candidate personally did from team results; do not invent a lesson or metric to complete the framework.

For a technical discussion, prepare the actual problem, constraints, alternatives, decision, verification, and supported outcome. For a leadership discussion, prepare the relevant people or delivery decision, the candidate's authority, stakeholder tradeoffs, and supported result. Label a proposed approach to a hypothetical problem as hypothetical.

## Honest gap framing

For a confirmed decisive gap, state what the candidate has done, the limit of that experience, and a credible future approach. Distinguish missing evidence from absent capability. Do not script an answer that disguises absent experience as expertise or describes a proposed action as an achievement.

Keep unresolved evidence questions outside any answer intended to be spoken. Omit unsupported claims from the usable draft rather than leaving editorial placeholders inside it.

## Questions for the interviewer

Choose two or three questions about outcomes, scope, team interfaces, current challenges, or decision authority that follow from the role. Adapt questions to what the available sources leave unknown. Examples of structure include asking what success would look like after six months or how a particular cross-team decision is made; these are not statements about this employer.

## Rehearsal

When mock practice is requested, ask one role-relevant question and wait. Give specific feedback after the answer unless the user requested feedback at the end. Continue with one follow-up or next question. Do not replace interactive practice with a complete report or answers written before the candidate responds.

For a static preparation guide, close with one practical action, such as rehearsing a chosen story and checking whether the personal contribution is clear. Do not claim a measured speaking duration from word count alone.
