# ATS and recruiter review

Use standard section headings, one logical reading order, reverse chronology within Experience, consistent dates, readable type, and concise bullets. Put role-relevant evidence early. Use natural keywords only for supported experience, preserving exact technology names where relevant.

## Content check

- Match every title, employer, date, credential, number, tool, and ownership claim to supplied evidence. Resolve conflicts or use a supported lower-specificity version.
- Keep the candidate's contribution distinct from a team's or employer's result. Recheck arithmetic if converting supplied measurements.
- Remove duplicated bullets, generic superlatives, keyword stuffing, editorial placeholders, and confirmation notes from submission-ready copy.
- Preserve factual job titles. A target role may appear as a clearly aspirational direction, not as past employment.
- Confirm that the first page shows the strongest relevant evidence without requiring a long tools list or exhaustive career history.

## Structure and file check

- Use real text, ordinary bullets, and standard headings such as Professional Summary, Experience, Projects, Skills, and Education. Persona-specific order may differ.
- Keep essential contact and career information in the document body. Do not rely on a header/footer, chart, icon, image, rating bar, or decorative table to convey it.
- Use a single-column flow. A narrow date gutter in the designed themes is acceptable only if dates remain associated with the right role in extraction order; otherwise put dates inline.
- For PDF output, extract the final text and confirm names, contact details, dates, roles, bullets, and sections appear completely and in order. Inspect the rendered pages separately.
- For DOCX output, use ordinary paragraphs, styles, and lists; avoid text boxes or layout tables for essential content. Follow the document tool's render-and-check process.

Describe the result as ATS-readable or ATS-friendly after these checks. Do not claim universal ATS compatibility, a guaranteed pass, a fabricated scan score, or a higher probability of being hired. A readable document is not evidence that any particular employer's system accepted it.
